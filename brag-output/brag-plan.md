# Brag Plan: FinTrack

## What is this app?
Project delivery and the money for it on one set of records — a live status
board sitting on the same rows as the invoices, with GST and TDS kept separate
and an AI analyst that shows the SQL behind every figure.

## The angle
Not "look at our features." The product exists because of one specific,
checkable defect in how this work is done in India: TDS is deducted by the
client *before* they pay, and almost every receivables report leaves it sitting
in the outstanding column. So the number you chase every month is bigger than
the number anyone owes you. Lead with that — it is a claim a viewer can verify
against their own ledger in about four seconds, and it is the reason the rest
of the product is shaped the way it is.

## Hook (first 2-3 seconds)
A figure in outstanding, counting. Then the correction lands on top of it:
the amount nobody actually owes. Numbers moving is the one thing a finance
audience cannot look away from, and the gap between the two is the whole pitch.

## Key moments (the middle)
- Delivery board and the money on the same card — a project's lane and what
  that project is owed, because they are the same record. This is the
  differentiator; most tools do one or the other and you reconcile by hand.
- Ageing bands with TDS pulled out as its own column, not buried in outstanding.
- The analyst answering a plain question with the compiled SQL printed
  underneath it. "It shows you the query it ran" is the trust claim, and it is
  the one thing a viewer will not believe unless they see it.

## Outro / punchline
The wordmark, then: "Drive the real thing. No signup, no demo call." The site
carries a working sandbox on invented data — that is the actual call to action,
and it is unusual enough to be worth saying out loud.

## User flow worth showing
Board → open a project → its outstanding, its ageing, the query behind it.
Entry, key action, result — all three exist in the public sandbox, so every
frame is real UI rather than a mock.

## Tone
- Preset: polished
- Creative direction: "a finance product that shows its working"
- Interpretation: restraint. No whip-pans, no confetti, no exclamation marks.
  Motion is used to direct the eye to a number and then get out of the way.
  The product's credibility is the pitch, so the edit must not undercut it.

## Format: landscape — 1920x1080
## Duration: 21s

- Card: #fffefa / #171817
- Accent: #2f5bff (light) / #5c84ff (dark)
- Positive: #15803d / #22c55e — Negative: #b91c1c / #f87171
- Text: #142033 / #f2f2ee
- Display font: Fraunces (variable 300-700)
- Body font: Outfit (variable 100-900)
- Wordmark: Jost Wordmark (subset to the eight letters of "FinTrack")
- Strongest visual element: the sandbox — real UI, invented numbers

## Share copy (draft)
Most receivables reports count TDS as money you are owed. It was deducted
before the client paid. FinTrack keeps delivery and the money on one set of
records, and shows you the query behind every figure.

## Audio direction
- Role: sparse professional accents
- Music: happy-beats-business-moves-vol-1 (120.19 BPM, beat grid from 3.02s)
- Music treatment: in at 0, low bed under the hook, no swell until the reveal
- Music cue guidance: preset cue file read. Strong cues at 17.02, 18.52, 20.02.
  Beat grid is a clean 0.50s at 120 BPM, so sequential card reveals sit on
  every second beat (1.0s apart) without drifting.
- Audio-reactive treatment: none. On a finance product, visuals pulsing to a
  beat reads as a crypto ad.
- SFX posture: sparse. One tick on the counter settling, one on the SQL line.
- Audio-coupled moments: the count-up settling, the ageing cards arriving,
  the query line printing.
- Restraint rule: music must never fight a number the viewer is reading. No
  riser into the outro, no sting on the logo.
